Your Weekly Mood Trends

This heatmap shows your own mood trends across the term, based on daily logs you submitted. Each colour reflects how you were feeling, making it easy to spot patterns, such as when you felt more positive or overwhelmed. By recognising when your energy typically dips, you can plan ahead, manage workload more intentionally, and take proactive steps to protect your wellbeing during busier periods. Hover over any day to revisit your mood entry, and explore connections between your schedule and emotions!

Student Mood vs School Week

This graph reveals how your students’ emotions shift throughout the term. You’ll notice that moods like happiness gradually decline, while negative emotions such as anger increase, especially in the middle and later weeks. This insight helps you anticipate when students may feel more emotionally drained, so you can check in, ease pressure where possible, or incorporate wellbeing-focused activities during high-stress periods. Hover over the graph for a more specifc look at the data.

Student Stress Level vs Hours of Sleep

This visualisation shows how closely your students’ stress levels are linked to their sleep. When they sleep less, their stress increases—and when they get more rest, stress drops. Recognising this pattern can help you consider how assessment schedules, homework, or classroom routines might be affecting sleep. With this data, you’re in a better position to adjust demands and support students in maintaining healthier habits. Hover over the graph for a more specfic look at the data.

Student Gender vs Stress

This graph compares stress levels between male and female students over the course of a week. You’ll see that both groups experience higher stress mid-week, but female students consistently report higher levels overall. This may prompt you to consider how your classroom environment and support strategies address the different needs of your students, and whether some may benefit from more targeted emotional check-ins or flexible learning approaches. Hover over the graph for a more specfic look at the data.

Student Grade Level vs Sleep

This pie chart shows how much sleep students in each year group are getting. You’ll notice that younger students, like those in Year 10, tend to sleep more, while older students in Years 11 and 12 get significantly less. This suggests that increasing academic pressure is impacting their rest. Knowing this, you can reflect on how much work you're assigning to senior students, and look for ways to encourage better time management and self-care in your teaching practice. Hover over the graph for a more specfic look at the data.
